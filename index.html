Line math

Fishing tackle is sold on three hopeful numbers. The spool promises yards measured with a line diameter you did not buy. The line promises pounds that survive until the first knot, which keeps only its own percentage of them. And the sinker drawer guesses at weight while the current quietly votes. Line math works all three honestly: spool capacity by real cross-section, breaking strength after the knot, and sinker weight from the depth you fish and the drift you fight. Exact arithmetic, every borrowed number labeled.

The three tools

  • Spool plan - the rated yards at the rated diameter become the honest yards of the line you actually bought.
  • Knot plan - the line's pound test through the knot's labeled percentage becomes the strength you really hold.
  • Sinker plan - the depth and the drift become the ounces that keep the bait where the fish are.

Labeled norms

Labeled norms: capacity scales with the square of the diameter, a quarter-ounce per mph of drift, and knot percentages as starting points you replace by reading your own boxes. Line ages in the sun and weakens at every nick, so retie often and test the knot, not the packet. Check local regulations for seasons, limits and tackle rules before you fish.
